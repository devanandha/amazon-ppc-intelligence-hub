# v8: Budget-aware decision and evidence-allocation study — protocol

**Status at protocol drafting: before seeing any v8 test-seed outcomes.** Exploratory, **not preregistered**, not real Amazon advertising evidence. This is an extension of the independently modelled but internally invented v7 portfolio simulator.

## Problem
The published v7 VOI score assumed a fixed no-auction binary decision and performed worse than a simple posterior-uncertainty ranking in all six simulated environments (at the 10% quota). We test two *distinct* changes: (A) value-of-information ranking for three actions with explicit budget share and (B) budget-aware follow-up action selection. We do not change the v7 environment after seeing v8 test results.

## Design fixed in advance
- Reuse the **unmodified v7 simulator** `independent_outcomes.make_cohort` with its same six predefined environments, auction response, attribution and budget mechanics. The simulator is distinct from the proposed scoring model but is not external/empirical validation.
- **Train:** synthetic seeds 32000–32011 (12 seeds) across *only* reference, increased auction CPC and reduced-budget conditions. **Held-out evaluation:** seeds 42000–42029 (30 seeds), evaluated across all six v7 environments. These seeds do not overlap v5/v7 or each other.
- 180 search terms/seed, 30 per campaign. Quotas exactly **5%, 10%, 20%** of terms; 10% is primary. All rankers use the same quota within a cohort. Fixed quota can force negative-value exploration and is not an optimal real deployment policy.
- Comparison rankers: random, fewest clicks, v7 profitability uncertainty, unchanged v5 one-step VOI, and new three-action budget-aware VOI ranking. All decision rankings use only historical click/order counts, CPC, AOV, campaign assignment and announced campaign budget. They never access true CVR, margin, realised auction shocks, demand, potential outcomes, or reporting randomness.
- New method estimates Beta(2,18) conversion posteriors, uses exact beta-binomial distribution for 10 future clicks and hypothetical three-action contributions; model assumptions are fixed 25% margin, 0.90 SCALE quality, 1.16 SCALE CPC, 38/53 click potential, equal per-term campaign budget entitlement, and 2 currency units probe overhead. **None of these assumptions was calibrated to real seller data**. Budget share and shadow-cost multiplier are heuristic, not learned optimal bidding.
- Rank weight `gamma` selected from **0, .20, .50** on training seeds ONLY to maximize average net synthetic portfolio profit in the *v8 shared budget-aware-action* setting and primary 10% quota over three named train environments. This optimization sees hidden training simulator outcomes; real-world tuning would need authorised labelled data. Other rankings have no extra outcome-tuned parameters.
- Evaluate each ranking under two shared action policies: (i) v7 fixed three-action heuristic unchanged, and (ii) v8 budget-aware three-action optimiser. Within an action-policy stratum, all rankers share the same action policy, simulator, budget limits, and synthetic realised randomness. This distinguishes effect of **ranking** from effect of **action policy**. Compare action policies at matched ranking/quota separately. Do not describe a combined gain as isolated algorithm superiority.
- Primary outcome: mean **simulated net contribution per term** (fictional units, higher better), paired by same seed/scenario. Report paired v8 rank minus uncertainty and v5 VOI, with descriptive 95% normal-approximation interval using variability over independent seeds. Also compare v8 action vs v7 action under the same v8 allocation ranking. These CIs are not adjusted for multiple comparisons or simulator uncertainty.
- Training-selected gamma and full seed-level data retained, including negative results, for reproducibility. Distinct test cohorts share structural generative assumptions and are *not* six independently sourced datasets.

## Important limitations
The budget-aware action value calculations use naive equal budget shares; real allocation is sequential and priority based. Assumed click opportunities, CPC uplift and quality factors may disagree with the simulator; a lower-level match is not independent real-world validation. There are no real campaigns, bid experiments, safety controls or causal sales-lift estimates. The method uses established Bayesian decision theory and does not establish new mathematical novelty. This implementation is not suitable for actual bid or keyword changes.
